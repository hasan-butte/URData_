Your data is your life, laid bare. You're data.

Ever wondered what price you're really paying to use your tech?

Overview

The technology you rely on is not entirely harmless. Odds are, your lifestyle is being recorded by the devices you use: an app logging your keystrokes, a search engine logging sensitive information you had to research, an operating system taking regular screenshots of your desktop, a network of license plate readers tracking your commute. This happens at every scale, from the device in your pocket to infrastructure you pass without noticing.

Your searches, purchases, location, health records, and relationships compose your identity. On its own, each device only picks up a slice of that. The real value comes from coupling: linking data across systems into a single profile that's more revealing than any one source alone, one that can be profited from. You are data.

The societal and ethical costs of this are real, not abstract. Your identity becomes more exposed, and your ability to make free, unmanipulated choices gradually erodes. These effects fall heaviest on communities already under the most scrutiny. As people learn how much is being collected, trust in the institutions behind it erodes too.

This site is built to explain the ethical, societal, and privacy implications of data aggregation. It documents how collection works in practice, examines what happens when it's allowed to continue, and closes with concrete steps to limit your exposure. No one has to give up technology. But everyone should understand what they're agreeing to when they use it.
