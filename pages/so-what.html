Why Does It Matter?

The collection of personal data at scale is not a neutral act. It is a system of extraction and commodification, in which power asymmetries between institutions and individuals produce exploitation at both a personal and societal scale, and in which mistrust and hesitation become the quiet cost of everyday life. Deontological ethics holds that people must be treated as ends in themselves, yet data collection treats every user as a resource to be mined. Utilitarian analysis offers no defense either: the benefits concentrate among a few corporations while the costs are diffused across billions. And the arrangement borrows the logic of a social contract: an implicit trust between institution and individual, one nobody actually negotiated. No reasonable, fully informed person would agree to these terms. What follows explores why.
